Design response · pass 03 · ceremony and voice

Pass 01 drew the map. Pass 02 built the chrome. Pass 03 gives the city a voice for its big moments.

Six surfaces that mark the passage of time: the newspaper that announces an era, the record book, the clock that says the city is alive, the events that happen on the map, the diagram on the platform wall, and a note on sound. Everything extends the pass-02 system in ui.css; the new parts live in ui-pass03.css, states are CSS, and the page re-themes live. Numbers and copy are the real build's — 22 achievements by their names, the six eras, all 59 stations. The schematic map is its own full-bleed page: open it here.

a

The era front page

the flagship · a layout system, not five posters

Each era transition becomes a period front page fed by live data: the year, a headline about the line that just opened, and the player's own numbers. It is one template with marked data slots — masthead, dateline, headline, lede, two or three stat callouts, and a motif slot that takes the era's line colour. The masthead never changes (a paper keeps its name); the era is carried by the motif colour and the dateline, so five eras and the ending are one design. The paper speaks in Libre Caslon — the city's voice — while every number stays in the game's mono. Switch eras to see the same template re-skin:

Achievements at scale

22 today, built to hold 100+

The pass-02 rows work at 22 and drown at 100. This is the categorised book: a header per category with a small glyph in the pass-01 icon grammar and a live count, then a grid of aims. Earned fills the mark green and states the aim in the past; locked stays fully legible because the hint is the point (nothing here is secret by default); hidden shows ??? until earned; a tiered family carries pips for how far up the magnitudes you are. The toast stays exactly as pass 02 built it. Real names and hints from the build:

Categories shown are a proposed grammar — building, service, riders, money, trust, history, night, endgame — you own the final set. Tiered families (the ride-count ladder: 100k → 1M → 10M) collapse three rows into one card with pips, which is most of how the set scales from 22 to 100 without a wall of text. Hidden entries are the only secrets; everything else advertises its aim, which is the pass-01 "the promise" rule applied to the record book.

The statistics office

a purchased reward · opens over the menu

Bought in-game, so it should feel earned: a page of graphs and records. d

The clock and the rush grade

240-second day · rendered as 24 hours

A small HUD clock says the city is alive: an analogue tick plus the hour, its phase in a word (the build's four phases — morning peak 06–12, midday, evening peak 18–24, night). During a peak a pulsing chip announces the rush; when it ends it resolves to a grade for the share of riders the network actually carried. The build already scores it A to E with a capped trust nod for a clean rush — so the grade is information plus a small reward, never punishment. E reads as "here is your ceiling," not "you failed."

Grades map to the build's thresholds (A ≥ 97% carried, B ≥ 90, C ≥ 75, D ≥ 50, E below) and the A/B trust nod is the real reward. Colour is meaning, not decoration: A/B proceed-green, C neutral, D amber, E red — but the word carries the tone. "Overwhelmed" states a fact about capacity; there is no fail state, so nothing here scolds. A three-station toy earns no grade at all (the build's 25-rider floor), so early play is never graded harshly.

e

Event language on the map

three marker families · pass-01 glyph grammar

Three new things happen on the map, and each needs to read at a glance against the station glyphs already there. An incident (signal failure, stalled train) is amber-family and resolvable — it pulses for attention, not alarm. A curiosity (a ghost station, an odd fact in the fabric of the city) is quiet and discoverable and must not read as a task, so it is a faint politic diamond that rewards noticing rather than demanding action. And the toasts — small facts and commuter postcards — sit on the map edge without competing with the achievement toast.

Each is the station ring plus one modifier, exactly the pass-01 rule: incident adds a pulsing amber halo and a warning tick, the ghost station is a mothballed cross, the curiosity steps outside the ring vocabulary on purpose (a diamond, not a circle) so it never looks like somewhere you were meant to build.

f

The schematic map

its own full-bleed page · both themes

The payoff of the whole fantasy — the diagram on the platform wall, drawn by the player — is a full surface that replaces the map rather than floating over it, so it gets its own page like the night basemap did. It is built from the real diagram coordinates for all 59 stations, in the Beck tradition (45° geometry, even tick spacing, interchange marks, terminus caps) but evoking the genre, never any operator's actual map. Sound direction

optional · art-directing the synthesised set

The dev ships code-synthesised effects, which keeps the zero-dependency and zero-licence rules. If the team wants a direction rather than defaults, here is one that matches the visual system: an instrument, not a jingle. Restrained, wooden, a period PA rather than a game.

Departure bell (AVGÅNG)two-tone, falling minor third · G→E · soft mallet, short decay
Fare landinga single warm tick, felt more than heard · pitch rises faintly with the amount
Era stinga held major chord, one per era, keyed to the era colour (warmer over time)
Achievementthe bell's third, resolved upward · a small confirmation, not a fanfare
Incidenta low, brief buzz · attention, never alarm — matches the amber halo
Ambient bed (optional)off by default · a distant platform hum that thickens with ridership

Principle: the network is the instrument. The more the city rides, the fuller the bed — sound as a readout, the way the number is. Any real audio asset ships CC0 or credited in About (owner rule, 2026-08-07); this direction assumes the synthesised path, so nothing here needs a licence line.

Back to the dev side

adoption, and what needs a ruling

Two files to take

ui-pass03.css (extends ui.css, new tokens at its top) and this page's demos. Same shape as pass 02: load tokens → tokens-ui → ui → ui-pass03. The schematic page carries its own diagram-drawing script since the coordinates live in data.js.

Data slots

The front page marks every bindable field with data-slot. The achievements grid, stat table and graph are rendered from small arrays in this page's script — swap the array for the real game state and the markup is unchanged.

Icons

Category glyphs and marker families are the pass-01 grammar (24 box, 2px stroke, currentColor), so they colour by state for free and extend by drawing one shape.

Rulings I need

Masthead name. "Tunnelbanebladet" is my placeholder and reads as invented, not as any real paper. Keep it, or give me the name you want set. Achievement categories. I proposed eight; confirm the grammar before I glyph all 100+. Font. Libre Caslon (OFL) for the paper — approve and I'll add the About credit line.

Light theme on the schematic

Both themes are drawn. The diagram is the one surface where light genuinely competes with dark — a white platform-map is its own tradition. My recommendation is on that page.

Not in this pass

Bulk upgrades, player-defined stop patterns and the while-you-were-away report are chrome the pass-02 panel already covers; say the word if any needs bespoke design. Synthesised sound stays code-side unless you want real assets.
