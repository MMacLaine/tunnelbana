Design response · pass 04 · negotiation

The player has been building the city. Pass 04 is where the city starts building back.

Five surfaces where the two negotiate: a council that spends trust on decisions rather than upgrades, districts that fill with warm light as they are served, a golden train worth catching, the depot a line calls home, and the small pieces that hold a session together. Everything extends the pass 02 and pass 03 layers in ui.css and ui-pass03.css; the new parts live in ui-pass04.css, states are CSS, the page re-themes live, and the copy obeys the 2026-08-08 voice rule. District names, the trust economy and Silverpilen are the real v12 build's.

a

The council

the flagship · a decision system, not a shop

The shop spends money on upgrades. The council spends trust on decisions, and a decision is different in kind: it is a choice the city remembers, it often rules out another, and it keeps paying an effect long after you take it. So the card is not a shop card. It wears trust's politic keyline as its family mark, it states its ongoing effect on its own face, and prerequisite decisions are wired together so a locked card shows you the choice that would open it. Four states carry the whole tree.

The four states

The tree, as an overlay · prerequisite paths light when satisfied

Charter · early
Corridor · mid
Mandate · late

Recommendation on where it lives: an overlay, like statistics, not a full-bleed moment like the front pages. A front page is the city announcing something to you and then getting out of the way; the council is a place you sit and weigh things with the map still in mind, so it should dim the map rather than replace it, and close back to exactly where you were. Prerequisite lines: drawn behind the cards, dashed and muted while unmet, solid politic once satisfied, so a locked card always points at the decision that frees it. Ongoing effects live on the taken card itself, because trust decisions are the one thing in the game you cannot undo, and the record of what you chose should stay visible.

b

Visible growth

a renderer treatment · the served city fills with light

Districts already grow in the sim toward a cap when service is good, and today the only sign is a number in the Network panel. Pass 04 makes it visible: warm window light accumulates inside the lit circles as a district fills, so the reward for good service is that the city you revealed becomes a city that is lived in. The rule that keeps it from fighting the map is that the light is warmer than anything else on the night map and it only ever appears inside an already-revealed circle, so it reads as depth, never as a new thing to chase. This is a renderer treatment, so the deliverable is this annotated reference plus the colour values.

Rendered as scattered warm points, not a fill: the number of lit windows is the district's living population against its cap (the sim's srcW over authored w × growthCap), so the eye counts density the way it counts a passenger queue. A faint warm wash (glow-core) sits under a dense cluster to bind the points together. It sits beneath lines, labels and station glyphs in the draw order, so nothing legible is ever obscured. On the light theme the points darken to amber so they still read as lit windows against a bright ground rather than washing out.

c

The golden train

a rare visitor · spotting, not a popup

A gold train appears now and then on a line. Catch it for a small bonus, miss it and nothing is lost. It has to read as something you spot rather than something the game demands, which means two rules. It must be unmistakably gold, and clearly not Silverpilen (the silver ghost train from pass 03 that you also catch at night), so the two never blur into one mechanic. And its attention cue is a slow soft pulse, the same family as an incident but warm and calm, never a hard blink or a modal.

The two night visitors, side by side

Both are the 1950 train shape from pass 01, so they belong to the same world. Gold gets a slow warm halo and rides any line; silver stays bare, unlit, cued by nothing, drifting the green line only. The halo is the whole difference in behaviour: the golden train wants to be seen, the ghost does not.

The bonus toast · quieter than an achievement

Golden trainYou caught it near Skanstull+240 kr, a small windfall
SilverpilenYou saw the ghost train slip through Slussen

The bonus toast is the pass-03 toast with a gold rail and gold name, one notch busier than a fact and well below the achievement toast, so catching the train feels like a wink from the city rather than a prize screen. Missing it produces no toast at all, because a message about a thing you failed to click is exactly the nagging the brief rules out.

d

The depot

a place the player sites · lowest-pressure item

A depot is home for a line's trains, with morning pull-outs. It is a building, not a stop, so its map glyph steps outside the station ring the way the pass-01 depot symbol already does (a rounded shed with rails), and its panel is the pass-02 anatomy with one thing the shop never needed: a stabled-versus-out bar, so you can see at a glance how much of the fleet is home. Siting one is a placement like a station, so it reuses the drag-and-snap language and the cost chip.

e

Small pieces

save slots, and how to add a station mid-line

Save-slot row · three slots on the menu

The slot is the pass-02 button frame with a big ghost numeral, the network's name, and the three facts that identify a save at a glance: the year reached, a landmark, and riders carried. The empty slot is dashed, the pass-02 promise state, so three slots read as one filled, one older, one waiting.

Insert a station · the midpoint affordance

A ghost node with a plus sits at a segment midpoint when you hover the line, using the amber "value / act here" language and the pass-01 selected-station ticks, so the gesture reads as "act on the line" before any tooltip. It is a good first gesture. If playtests show players hunting for midpoints, the fallback is a small plus that rides the cursor along the whole segment rather than sitting only at its centre, which is a one-line change to where the affordance is anchored. Worth confirming before players are trained on the midpoint.

Back to the dev side

adoption, and what needs a ruling

Two files to take

ui-pass04.css (extends ui.css and ui-pass03.css, new tokens at its top) and this page's demos. Load order is tokens → tokens-ui → ui → ui-pass03 → ui-pass04.

Council data

Each card is markup plus a data-state. The tree here renders prerequisite wires from a small array in this page's script; swap it for the council's real graph and the card markup is unchanged. A decision needs an id, a cost, a state, a one-line effect, and its prerequisite ids.

Growth is renderer, not DOM

Section b is a reference plus four glow tokens and the draw-order rule (under lines and labels, inside revealed circles only, count of points tied to srcW over cap). Nothing to port as a component.

Rulings I need

Council home. I recommend an overlay over a full-bleed moment. Confirm and I lock the layout to it. Insert gesture. Midpoint click is good; approve it or the along-segment fallback before players learn it. Council categories. I used growth, corridor, service as the tiers; confirm the grammar before the tree grows over releases.

Both themes

Council, depot, slots and toasts re-theme with the toggle. Growth carries its own light-theme override so lit windows stay legible on a bright ground. The golden train is gold in both, since gold is the point.

Not in this pass

The golden train's spawn cadence and the depot's pull-out timing are sim balance, not design. The while-you-were-away report is still parked. Sound for the golden train would follow the pass-03 direction if you want it.
